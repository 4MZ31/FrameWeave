PushFPS 3.1 setup guide: the best settings to start from
==========================================================

PushFPS is built on Veyra by Likely7. This guide is also on the website:
https://dosukasol.github.io/PushFPS/#guide

Contents
  1. Start here: recommended PushFPS settings
  2. Then tune it to your liking
  3. Console settings (PS5, PS5 Pro, Xbox Series X|S, Switch, Switch 2)
  4. Windows settings
  5. When to use HDR (playing, recording, streaming)
  6. Quick checklist


1. START HERE: RECOMMENDED PUSHFPS SETTINGS
-------------------------------------------

Get the game on screen first, then set these in the Quick setup (Home or Pro mode):

  Console ................ your console (each console remembers its own choices)
  Game frame rate ........ 30 for most games (GTA VI), 40 for 40 FPS modes, 60 for performance modes
  Target frame rate ...... 60 FPS on a 60 Hz monitor, 120 FPS on 120 Hz and faster
                           (per refresh rate: "Which target for your monitor" below)
  Game preset ............ pick the game if it is listed (GTA VI has one): it sets the HUD boxes
  Smooth frame pacing .... ON (default): every generated frame is shown once
  Frame Pacing Repair .... ON: evens out uneven 30 FPS games before frames are generated
  Live HUD Lock .......... ON: keeps text, crosshairs and the minimap sharp
  Adaptive multiplier .... ON for games that dip below their frame rate
  AI picture enhancement . OFF to start (it uses a lot of GPU). Turn it on later if you have headroom
  Lowest latency ......... ON for action games. It changes nothing about picture quality

Then click "Optimise for my PC" with the game on screen (goal: Balanced). PushFPS tests the frame
generation methods your PC has on your real picture and keeps the best. "Undo" puts everything back.

Which target for your monitor
  Pick a target your monitor's refresh rate divides evenly, so every frame stays on screen equally
  long. That is why 120 beats 144: 144 is not a whole multiple of 30 or 60. "Match monitor" sets the
  target to your monitor's own rate.

  Monitor      30 FPS game (GTA VI)            60 FPS game                  Good to know
  60 Hz        60 (2x)                         Off                          Use Lowest latency only
  75/100 Hz    60 (2x)                         Off                          G-SYNC/FreeSync on, or Windows at 60 Hz
  120 Hz       120 (4x)                        120 (2x)                     The sweet spot for console games
  144/165 Hz   120 (4x)                        120 (2x)                     G-SYNC/FreeSync on, or Windows at 120 Hz
  180 Hz       120 (4x) or Match monitor (6x)  Match monitor (3x)           180 = 6 x 30 = 3 x 60
  240 Hz       120 (4x) or Match monitor (8x)  120 (2x) or Match mon. (4x)  Start at 120
  360 Hz       120 (4x)                        120 (2x) or Match mon. (6x)  360 = 3 x 120

  Which multipliers your method offers:
    2x ......... every method
    3x and 4x .. NVIDIA Video Frame Generation, NVIDIA Frame Generation for games on RTX 50, ANR, Intel XeSS
    6x ......... NVIDIA Video Frame Generation, NVIDIA Frame Generation for games on RTX 50, ANR
    8x ......... NVIDIA Video Frame Generation only (RTX 40/50)
  AMD FSR stops at 2x, so a 30 FPS game goes to 60. More generated frames per real frame show more
  wobble in fast motion: if you see it, step down to 120 or 60. "Optimise for my PC" tests 120 and
  your monitor's rate when your PC can reach it, and keeps what passes.


2. THEN TUNE IT TO YOUR LIKING
------------------------------

Change one thing at a time, play a minute, then decide.

  If you see ...                              Try ...
  Smeared HUD or text in fast motion          Live HUD Lock on, use the game preset or Automatic HUD
                                              detection, or a lower target (60 instead of 120)
  Wobbly edges or artifacts in fast motion    A lower target, or a higher frame generation quality in Pro mode
  Stutter or uneven motion                    Smooth frame pacing on, G-SYNC/FreeSync on in your driver,
                                              capture card set to exactly 60 FPS, fullscreen (F11)
  The game feels slow to react                Lowest latency on, 120 Hz console output (if your capture
                                              card takes it), or Target "Off" for competitive games
  Soft or blocky picture                      AI picture enhancement on (RTX 40/50), or NVIDIA Image
                                              Scaling sharpening in Pro mode
  Frame rate drops with AI enhancement on     AI enhancement off, or a lower target
  Washed-out or too dark picture              Match the colour settings in the capture dialog to the
                                              console: HDR or SDR, Limited or Full range
  Black picture from a PlayStation            Turn HDCP off on the PS5 (see below)

Want the best picture rather than the lowest delay? Use "Optimise for my PC" with the goal "Best
picture". Want the lowest delay? Goal "Lowest latency" plus the Lowest latency switch.


3. CONSOLE SETTINGS
-------------------

All consoles
  - Connect the console's HDMI out to the capture card's input, and the card to a USB 3 port directly
    on the PC (or a PCIe slot).
  - Output 60 Hz. Use 120 Hz only if your capture card captures 120 Hz at that resolution (for example
    Elgato 4K X, 4K S or HD60 X at 1080p or 1440p): each frame then arrives about 8 ms sooner.
  - Variable refresh rate (VRR) OFF on the console. Capture needs a steady 60 or 120 Hz signal.
  - Resolution: what your capture card captures at 60 FPS (4K if it does 4K60, otherwise 1080p or 1440p).
  - In-game: a mode locked to 30 FPS gives the sharpest picture, and PushFPS adds the smoothness.
    A 60 FPS performance mode gives less delay.

PlayStation 5 and PS5 Pro
  - Settings > System > HDMI > Enable HDCP: OFF (required, otherwise the capture card shows black).
  - Settings > Screen and Video > Video Output > Resolution: match your capture card.
  - 120 Hz Output: Automatic only if your capture card captures 120 Hz; otherwise Off.
  - VRR: Off.
  - HDR: see section 5. "On When Supported" for HDR play, "Off" for streaming and SDR clips.
  - RGB range: Automatic, and match it in PushFPS's capture dialog (Limited or Full).

Xbox Series X|S
  - Settings > General > TV & display options:
      Resolution: match your capture card.
      Refresh rate: 60 Hz (120 Hz only if your capture card captures 120 Hz).
  - Video modes: Allow variable refresh rate OFF, Allow 24 Hz OFF. HDR10 and Auto HDR: see section 5.
  - Xbox games are not HDCP protected, so nothing else is needed.

Nintendo Switch
  - Docked. System Settings > TV Output: TV resolution 1080p, RGB range Automatic (match it in the
    capture dialog).

Nintendo Switch 2
  - Docked, 60 Hz output. TV resolution 1080p, or 4K if your capture card captures 4K60.
  - HDR: see section 5.
  - Switch games are not HDCP protected.

Videos on your PC (YouTube in a browser, video apps, files): use Video playback mode (the play
button in the top bar). Pick the window, switch on Smoother and Sharper, press F11.

No capture card? Streaming sources work too (PS5 Remote Play, Moonlight from a Sunshine PC, Xbox).
For PS5 Remote Play pick 1080p 60 FPS with H.265 (the default for new pairings).


4. WINDOWS SETTINGS
-------------------

  Hardware-accelerated GPU scheduling ... ON  (Settings > System > Display > Graphics > Advanced
                                               graphics settings). Required for NVIDIA's frame
                                               generation; on by default in Windows 11. Restart after
                                               changing it.
  Monitor refresh rate .................. the highest your monitor has (Settings > System > Display >
                                          Advanced display)
  G-SYNC / FreeSync ..................... ON in the NVIDIA Control Panel or AMD Software, for
                                          fullscreen and windowed mode
  Windows HDR ........................... see section 5
  Game Mode ............................. ON (the Windows default)
  Graphics driver ....................... up to date
  Power ................................. laptops: plugged in, "Best performance" power mode
  Microphone (for recordings/streams) ... Settings > Privacy & security > Microphone > allow desktop
                                          apps to access your microphone
  Other apps ............................ close OBS (unless you use the PushFPS OBS plugin) and the
                                          capture card's own software before connecting
  PushFPS window ........................ play fullscreen (F11): it skips the desktop compositor


5. WHEN TO USE HDR
------------------

HDR only works end to end when every step is HDR: console, capture card, Windows and your monitor.
One SDR step means SDR.

  What you do                          Console HDR   Windows HDR   PushFPS
  -----------------------------------  -----------   -----------   ---------------------------------------
  Play on an HDR monitor               On            On            Default (HDR output). Capture card set
  (capture card captures HDR)                                      to HDR in the capture dialog
  Play on an SDR monitor               Off           Off           Default
  Record clips to share (Discord,      Off           Off           SDR recording (H.264). Or keep the
  TikTok, Shorts, Reels, most editors)                             console on HDR and turn on "Show as
                                                                   SDR" in the capture dialog
  Record in HDR (YouTube HDR,          On            On            Needs an NVIDIA graphics card: the file
  HDR video editing)                                               is HEVC 10-bit HDR
  Go live (Twitch, YouTube, Kick,      Off           Off           Streaming needs an SDR picture;
  TikTok)                                                          PushFPS asks you to turn HDR off first
  Vertical comparison clips            Off           Off           SDR gives a file every phone app shows
                                                                   correctly

Tips
  - If the picture looks grey or washed out, one step disagrees: check the console's HDR setting, the
    capture dialog's colour settings and Windows HDR.
  - Switching Windows HDR changes the whole desktop. Turn it on only for HDR play and HDR recordings.
  - Xbox Auto HDR adds HDR to SDR games: leave it off when you want SDR clips or streams.


6. QUICK CHECKLIST
------------------

  [ ] HDCP off (PlayStation)
  [ ] Console at 60 Hz (120 Hz only with a 120 Hz capture card), VRR off
  [ ] Capture card on a USB 3 port directly on the PC, set to exactly 60 FPS (or 120)
  [ ] Quick setup: console, game frame rate 30, target from the monitor table (60 or 120), game preset
  [ ] Smooth frame pacing, Frame Pacing Repair and Live HUD Lock on
  [ ] Optimise for my PC with the game on screen
  [ ] Lowest latency on for action games
  [ ] G-SYNC/FreeSync on, monitor at its highest refresh rate, fullscreen (F11)
  [ ] HDR only when every step is HDR; SDR for streams and shared clips

Problems? In PushFPS: Settings > Report a problem copies diagnostic information.
