## What's new in 4.1

4.1 does two things. It takes back what 4.0 got wrong, so the picture with frame generation is what it
was in 3.2.10 again. And it adds what people asked for: a check that tells you which setting costs you
quality, help with capture cards, more for recording, reading and translating the text on screen, and a
cleaner interface.

If something is worse for you than in 3.2.10, please tell us with a performance report
(*Settings → Performance report → Copy*).

### The artifacts 4.0 brought are gone

4.0 gave some of you artifacts around characters and objects, a softer picture, and poles and thin
structures cutting in and out while the camera moved. Two changes of 4.0 caused it, and both are taken
back.

- **"Keep the HUD sharp" is off unless you switch it on.** In 4.0 it was on for everyone. It keeps a pixel
  that has the same value in two real frames, and in a real game that is not the same as standing still:
  on textured pictures it replaced 5 to 38 % of every generated frame with pixels of the older frame. If
  the switch was on in your settings, 4.1 switches it off once and says so. It is still there as an
  option; for a HUD, the game presets, HUD protection and Live HUD Lock are the tools.
- **Duplicate removal on *60 → 30* is 3.2.10's again.** 4.0 passed over a frame when the picture had
  changed only a little (a slight turn of the camera, a small moving thing), and those were real frames of
  the game. On a steady 30 FPS game it handed on 22 to 27 frames a second instead of 30.

With both back, the frames a frame generation method is given, and what is done with the frames it makes,
is the same code as in 3.2.10. That holds for every method: NVIDIA's, FSR and PushFlow. We compared it
line by line; we have not yet seen it on a real card ourselves.

If you use NVIDIA's frame generation and still see broken edges around things that move: its quality
(Low, Medium, High) is now a row in quick setup. It starts on Medium as it always did; on a card with room,
High is worth trying.

### Check my setup

A new tile on the home page (and in *Settings*). It looks at the picture that is running for a few
seconds and says what costs you sharpness, smoothness or sound, each with the one change that answers it:

- frame generation that is on but shows no generated frames, or falls behind;
- AI picture enhancement worked out at 1080p on a 1440p or 4K picture;
- NVIDIA's frame generation on a lower quality than the card has room for, or PushFlow on a lower tier;
- a target the screen cannot show, or one that cannot be paced evenly;
- a capture card that sends a compressed picture where it has an uncompressed one;
- a capture card connected without its sound.

Nothing changes until you press a button, every change can be undone from the same row, and a change with
a price says its price.

### A sharper picture

- **AI picture enhancement at the picture's own size.** It worked at up to 1080p, also for a 4K picture.
  *Quick setup → Enhancement size* now offers 1080p (as always), 1440p and full size. Full size is sharper
  at 4K and about four times the work: if frame generation starts skipping frames, go back.
- **Sharper scaling to the window.** A picture that is not the window's size is now resized with a finer
  filter, for every source. A 4K picture on a 4K screen is the same pixels as before. It is a switch:
  *Settings → Playback → Sharp scaling*.
- **A sharper capture format, when your card has one.** Many USB cards offer the uncompressed format only
  up to 30 FPS at 1080p and send a compressed picture at 60. The capture dialog now says so and offers the
  switch. It is offered, never done for you: at 30 the card hands over half the frames of a 60 FPS game.

### Capture cards

- **The capture card is chosen first, not the webcam.** On a PC where no card was used yet, PushFPS took
  the first video device Windows listed, which on a laptop is the webcam.
- **Help when no card is found.** The capture dialog now says what to check (a USB 3 port, the maker's
  driver, another program holding the card) and shows when Windows' camera permission is switched off,
  with a button to its page. Windows counts a capture card as a camera.

### Recording and streaming

- **Mark a moment while you record.** *F6* (or the button) notes the time. The times are in a text file
  with the recording's name beside it, so the moment is easy to find when you edit.
- **A vertical copy beside your recording.** Switch it on in the record dialog and a 1080 × 1920 file is
  recorded next to the wide one, filled or fitted, for TikTok, Shorts and Reels.
- **Recordings in parts by size**, not only by time.
- **Comparison clips name your console**: PS5, PS5 Pro, Xbox Series X|S, Switch or Switch 2 instead of
  "Console".
- **PushFPS as a camera.** *Settings → Recording → Virtual camera* makes "PushFPS Virtual Camera" a video
  device other programs can choose: the whole processed picture, without full screen.
- **Local control.** A button box (Stream Deck and the like) or a script on the same PC can start and stop
  a recording, save the replay, mark a moment, take a screenshot and switch a scene. It is off unless you
  switch it on, listens on this PC only, and every request needs a token. How to use it is in
  `docs/LOCAL-CONTROL.md`.

### Read the text on the picture

A menu in another language, a line of dialogue: *F4* (or *Settings → Playback → Read the text on the
picture*) takes one frame of the picture and has Windows read the text on it. You can copy the text or
translate it.

- The reading happens on your PC, with the languages Windows has text recognition for. To read Japanese or
  Korean, add the language in Windows' language settings first.
- Only the picture PushFPS shows is read, only when you ask, and the frame is deleted once it is read.
- A translation has its own button. With the local model the text stays on your PC (a download of about
  1.1 GB, once). With an online engine the dialog says, before you press it, that the text will be sent
  there.
- It also works with an HDR picture.

### Sound

- **PS5 Remote Play: the sound no longer runs seconds behind.** The sound could play about two seconds
  after the picture. Automatic sync had nothing to check its estimate of the picture's delay against for
  Remote Play; it now knows when each shown frame really arrived, as for every other source.

### Screens

- **Moving PushFPS to another screen no longer drops to the game's frame rate.** A 120 FPS target on a
  60 Hz screen with smooth frame pacing asked for 120 pictures where 60 can be shown; the generated frames
  came late and were dropped. A target the screen cannot show is now put out at the screen's refresh rate,
  and at your target again when the window is back on the fast screen.
- **A maximise button** in the headers of Professional and Streaming mode.
- **The update notice is at the bottom** of the window, no longer over the navigation.
- **The graphics card's load is counted as Task Manager counts it.** The figure could read 100 % on a card
  that was a quarter busy. Nothing in PushFPS acted on it; it was the readout that was wrong.

### Graphics cards without NVIDIA's features

- **Choose the method on the home page.** PushFlow, FSR, AMD FRC and the others were only in Professional
  mode. An AMD card starts on FSR; if you see ghosting there, the row says that PushFlow is there to try,
  and *Optimise for my PC* measures every method on your card. Nothing is switched for you.
- **The low latency switch works on every card.** On AMD and Intel cards it was greyed out. There it is
  now the low queue: one frame waits for the screen instead of three.
- What we could not do: FSR 4's frame generation is limited to RX 9000 cards by AMD, and FSR's ghosting
  comes from how it is fed (motion estimated from video), which needs an AMD card to work on.

### A cleaner interface

- **Quick setup is in six sections** (console and game, frame generation, picture, HUD, latency and sound,
  connecting). A section folds by its title and stays folded.
- **Each setting is on a page once.** Professional and Streaming mode no longer repeat what their own
  lists and buttons already have.
- **More colours**: six backgrounds (graphite, black, light, midnight, plum, forest) and seven accents,
  every pair checked for readable contrast, plus a **high contrast** look.
- **A larger latency readout**, in two more sizes.
- **Settings are regrouped**: recording, comparison clips, instant replay and the virtual camera have a
  page of their own.

### Watching video

- **Dolby Vision** profiles 5, 7, 8 and 10 are converted, following the picture's own brightness data
  scene by scene.
- **Live translated subtitles** (Windows 11): what is said in a video, read by Windows' live captions and
  translated on the picture. The local model is the default; an online engine is your own choice.
- **Network streams over RTP and UDP**, and **live timeshift** for a stream that is running.
- **A crop editor for screen capture**, a badge that shows the source's format when it starts, and
  **DualSense trigger effects** in PS5 Remote Play.
- **Automatic multiplier per session** (off unless you switch it on): the multiplier follows the content's
  frame rate.

### Linux and macOS

People who tried the macOS version found it slower to react than Windows. Three causes were found and
fixed:

- Every session started with extra delay (up to 40 ms) that took over five minutes to go away.
- After a slow moment the app kept its extra margin for minutes. It now gives it back within seconds, and
  only when it has measured that the margin is spare.
- The motion of a pair of frames waited for a colour conversion it did not need.

Also new there: **Lowest latency** (one tap), **lip sync by hand**, **connect when PushFPS opens**, the
capture format remembered per card, and bars / fill / stretch for a picture of another shape than the
window. Frame generation on these systems finds motion on the processor, so its delay stays above the
Windows version's. Recording and going live are still Windows only.

### What was tested where

Everything in 4.1 was built and passed the automatic tests: the unit tests, the interface tests, and the
graphics tests on a software renderer. The Linux and macOS engine changes passed their tests, including a
simulated slow machine.

Not seen on real hardware by us yet, so your reports matter most here:

- that the artifacts of 4.0 are gone on a real card (it is the same code as 3.2.10, compared line by line);
- NVIDIA's frame generation on High, full-size enhancement at 4K, and the sharper scaling on a real screen;
- reading a real game's text, in SDR and HDR, and the local translation model;
- a vertical copy recorded beside a recording (two encodes at once), clip markers and local control;
- the virtual camera, Dolby Vision and live translated subtitles;
- the capture card choices on a PC with a real card, Remote Play's sound on a console, the move between
  two screens, the new rows on an AMD card, and the delay on a real Mac.
