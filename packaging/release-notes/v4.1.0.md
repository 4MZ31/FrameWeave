## What's new in 4.1

4.1 is mostly requests from people who use PushFPS: other screens, other graphics cards, other systems.

### Fewer artifacts around things that move ("Keep the HUD sharp")

4.0's new HUD rule also acted along the border of every larger still region: the sky beside a moving head,
a wall behind a moving object. Frame generation leaves a soft trail there, and the rule replaced part of it
with real pixels, block by block: around everything that moved there were hard, broken pieces.

The rule now only keeps small still things in a moving picture: text, thin bars, icons, a crosshair. The
border of a larger still region is left to frame generation again, as before 4.0. What counts as "small"
follows the picture's size, so it is the same at 1080p and at 4K.

It is still on by default, and still its own switch (*Quick setup → Keep the HUD sharp*). If you see
artifacts around moving things in 4.1, switch it off and tell us: that is the first thing we want to know.

### PS5 Remote Play: the sound no longer runs seconds behind

In Remote Play the sound could play about two seconds after the picture. Automatic sync worked out how long
the picture takes from time stamps PushFPS estimates itself, and for Remote Play it had nothing to check
them against. When they were off (a session's first sound arriving late is enough), the sound was held back
by the error. It now knows when each shown frame really arrived, as it does for every other source, and
does not believe time stamps that do not fit.

### Moving PushFPS to another screen no longer drops to the game's frame rate

A 120 FPS target on a 60 Hz screen, with smooth frame pacing on, asked for 120 pictures where 60 can be
shown. The generated frames came late and were dropped, and what stayed was the game's own 30.

Now a target the screen cannot show is put out at the screen's refresh rate: 60 on the 60 Hz screen, and
120 again when the window is back on the fast one. Your target stays chosen, and quick setup says what is
put out. Without smooth frame pacing nothing changes.

### Graphics cards without NVIDIA's features

- **Choose the method on the home page.** PushFlow, FSR, AMD FRC and the others were only in Professional
  mode. An AMD card starts on FSR; if you see ghosting there, the row now says that PushFlow is there to
  try, and *Optimise for my PC* measures every method on your card. Nothing is switched for you.
- **The low latency switch works on every card.** On AMD and Intel cards it was greyed out ("needs an
  NVIDIA card"). There it is now the low queue: one frame waits for the screen instead of three.
- What we could not do: FSR 4's frame generation is limited to RX 9000 cards by AMD, and FSR's ghosting
  comes from how it is fed (motion estimated from video), which needs an AMD card to work on.

### AI picture enhancement at the picture's own size

The enhancement works at up to 1080p, also for a 4K picture. *Quick setup → Enhancement size* now offers
1080p (as always), 1440p and full size. Full size is sharper at 4K and about four times the work: if
frame generation starts skipping frames, go back.

### Small things you asked for

- **A maximise button** in the headers of Professional and Streaming mode.
- **Comparison clips name your console**: PS5, PS5 Pro, Xbox Series X|S, Switch or Switch 2 (the one
  chosen in quick setup) instead of "Console".
- **The update notice is at the bottom** of the window, no longer over the navigation.

### Linux and macOS

People who tried the macOS version found it slower to react than Windows. Three causes were found and
fixed:

- Every session started with extra delay (up to 40 ms) that took over five minutes to go away.
- After a slow moment the app kept its extra margin for minutes. It now gives it back within seconds, and
  only when it has measured that the margin is spare.
- The motion of a pair of frames waited for a colour conversion it did not need.

Also new there: **Lowest latency** (one tap), **lip sync by hand**, **connect when PushFPS opens**, the
capture format remembered per card, and bars / fill / stretch for a picture of another shape than the
window. Frame generation on these systems finds motion on the processor, so its delay stays above the
Windows version's. Recording and going live are still Windows only.

### What was tested where

The Windows changes passed the build's automatic tests on a software renderer; the Linux and macOS engine
changes passed its tests, including a simulated slow machine, with the thread sanitizer. Not seen on real
hardware yet: the tightened HUD rule, the move between two screens, Remote Play's sound on a console, the
new rows on an AMD card, full-size enhancement at 4K, and the delay on a real Mac. If something is worse for you than in 4.0, please tell us with a performance
report (*Settings → Performance report → Copy*).
