## What's new in 3.2.2: tune AI picture enhancement, ultrawide screens, 90 FPS, a crash notice and a graphics card test

### The look of AI picture enhancement

AI picture enhancement reworks light, colour and fine detail. How far it should go is a matter of taste, so the quick setup now has controls for it, shown while AI picture enhancement is on:

- **Enhancement look**: Subtle, Balanced or Strong. Strong is the full effect and what PushFPS has always used, so nothing changes until you pick another. **Reset** returns to it.
- **Lighting**: how far the AI reworks light and colour. At 0% the original's light and colours stay.
- **Detail**: how much fine texture, contact shadow and reflection the AI adds.
- **Compare with the original**: splits the picture while you tune, original on the left, enhanced on the right. Frame generation pauses while it is on, so it turns itself off after two minutes, and when you turn the enhancement off or leave the page.

### Fine-tune: shape what the enhancement changes

Asked for by users: the enhancement is applied as a change on top of the console's picture, and **Fine-tune** (under the look, folded until you open it) shapes that change without touching the picture itself.

| Slider | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Edit strength** | How much of the change is applied: 0% none, 100% as the model made it, 200% double |
| **Brightening**, **Darkening** | How much of the change that makes pixels brighter, or darker, is kept |
| **Max brightening**, **Max darkening** | A soft limit on how far any pixel may get brighter or darker. 0% is no limit |
| **Shadows**, **Midtones**, **Highlights** | How much of the change is kept in the dark, middle and bright parts of the picture |
| **Colour change** | How far the enhancement changes how strong colours are. 0% keeps the console's colours |
| **Hue shift** | How far it may turn hues. 0% keeps the console's hues |

With everything at its default nothing extra is done, so the picture is exactly what it was. The look and the fine-tuning are saved with every console and game profile. Fine-tune works with every enhancement runtime; the look's own controls are hidden with the AMD enhancement runtime, which has no such values.

### Ultrawide screens

A console sends a 16:9 picture whatever screen it ends up on. On a 21:9 or 32:9 screen the quick setup now shows **Wide-screen fill**:

- **Bars**: the whole picture, black bars at the sides (as before).
- **Ambient**: the whole picture, not cut and not distorted; the bars become a soft, dimmed extension of the picture itself.
- **Crop**: the screen filled, with about a quarter of the picture's height cut on 21:9 (half on 32:9).
- **Stretch**: the screen filled, with the picture pulled wider.

None of them shows more of the game: the console does not render it. Recordings and streams are not affected, and recordings have two new sizes, 3440 × 1440 and 2560 × 1080. Ambient fill is not available with XeSS frame generation.

### 90 FPS on 90 Hz screens

Asked for by a user: on a 90 Hz screen the target frame rate now offers **90 FPS** in place of 120, three frames for every frame of a 30 FPS game. Game presets pick it automatically there. (A 60 FPS game cannot reach 90 evenly, so for those the choice is not offered.)

### If PushFPS closes unexpectedly

Until now PushFPS only mentioned a crash at the next start when it recognised a known cause. Now it always does, with a summary you can copy: the version, where it stopped, your graphics card, what was playing and which methods were on, and whether **Optimise for my PC** was testing something. The summary contains no Windows user name, folder path or stream address. The crash file in the log folder does contain your user name, so the notice says to send that one privately, and PushFPS never sends anything by itself.

### Test my graphics card

*Settings → About → Test my graphics card* measures how fast PushFlow runs on your card at 1080p and 4K, on all three tiers, and says what that means: which tier reaches 60 or 120 FPS for a 30 FPS game. It takes about a minute, with nothing playing. The result (card name, memory, PushFPS version, Windows build and timings, nothing personal) can be copied and sent to us. PushFlow's speed has so far only been measured on a software renderer, so these are the first real numbers.

### GTA VI preset

The GTA VI preset now protects three HUD areas instead of two: the minimap bottom left, the health and focus bars top left, and the weapon, wanted level and cash top right. Which corner holds what comes from the footage Rockstar showed before release; the exact edges are still estimates, so check them with **Adjust HUD areas** once you play. The preset also turns on the adaptive multiplier, because reviews found the base PS5 dipping below 30 FPS in busy scenes.

### PushFlow: a crash when it was shut down

A crash report from Xbox streaming ended inside ONNX Runtime, the library that runs PushFlow's network, at the moment PushFlow was shut down to apply a setting. That is what **Optimise for my PC** does between every two tests, and what a stream does when it rebuilds shortly after connecting.

Read against the ONNX Runtime PushFPS ships, the report shows a fault in how that library's DirectML part ends a session: it lets go of its pool of graphics buffers first, and only then releases the buffers still waiting in its queue. A buffer released that late goes back to a pool that no longer exists. PushFlow now does two things about it:

- before a session closes, it lets the library finish its queued work and release those buffers while the pool is still there;
- it keeps the pool alive itself until the session is gone.

Since 3.1.7 this fault no longer ended PushFPS at once (it was caught and PushFlow reported a failure), but it left that session's memory behind. With these two changes a late release finds its pool still there. This changes how PushFlow shuts down for every source, capture cards included; nothing else about capture cards changes.

### How it was tested

On GitHub's Windows machines, which have no graphics card, so everything that draws ran on Windows' software renderer:

- **Fine-tune.** The shader that ships was run on test pictures and compared with reference maths for eleven settings, in SDR and HDR. The first run found a real fault before release: with a 5 % darkening limit, pixels darkened by up to 5.7 %. The limits were rewritten and now hold at 5.0 %. With every slider at its default the result is identical to 3.2.0's.
- **Ambient fill.** The blurred backdrop was checked in a test, and looked at in the real program playing a test picture in a window wider than it: the bars carry a dimmed, soft copy of the picture; with the choice off they are black as before.
- **Crash notice.** The summary's privacy rules are unit-tested (a user name, path or address in the crash handler's note never reaches the summary). The notice was looked at in the real program, started with a note as the crash handler writes it. A real crash was not provoked.
- **The quick setup, presets and dialogs**: 40 quick setup tests (90 FPS, wide-screen fill, fine-tune and the look among them), 23 preset tests, 7 for the crash notice and the card test.
- **PushFlow's shutdown**: started, run and shut down at once 24 times in four ways, with no fault. This did **not** reproduce the crash: the old shutdown also ran clean there. It shows the new shutdown works, not that the crash is gone.
- The installed program started and every main page opened without interface errors.

**Not tested on real hardware:**

- Fine-tune and the look with the real AI picture enhancement (it needs an NVIDIA card; the tests use made-up pictures in its place).
- Ambient fill on a real ultrawide screen, in HDR, and how it looks with a real game.
- 90 FPS on a real 90 Hz screen.
- The graphics card test on a real card: on the software renderer it would only measure the processor.
- The GTA VI preset on the game itself, which is not out yet.
- The PushFlow shutdown fix on a real graphics card or with a real console stream. If **Optimise for my PC** still stops PushFPS for you, the new crash notice shows what to send.

**Windows Defender note.** Defender may flag new PushFPS downloads as `Trojan:Win32/Tecabans.STV!cl`. This is a false positive from its cloud check on new, unsigned files, and it has been reported to Microsoft. Always check the zip against `SHA256SUMS.txt` on this page.
