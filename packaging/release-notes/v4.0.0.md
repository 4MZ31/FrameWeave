## What's new in 4.0

4.0 is about how the picture and the sound feel while you play: less ghosting in the HUD, a steadier
rhythm for 30 FPS games, sound you can line up with the picture, and a set of reliability fixes.

**Read this first:** everything below passed the automatic tests of the Windows build, which run on a
software renderer. **None of it has been run on a real graphics card or a real capture card by us yet.**
The section at the end says what was tested where. If something is worse for you than in 3.2.10, please
tell us with a performance report (*Settings → Performance report → Copy*).

### The HUD stays sharp with frame generation (on by default)

Frame generation works out one motion for every place of the picture. Where a HUD stands still in front
of a world that moves, the HUD's pixels were dragged along with the world: text, bars and the crosshair
smeared, doubled or vanished in the generated frames. That happened with every method.

Now a pixel that is the same in both real frames, and stands in a surrounding that mostly moves, keeps
its real value in the generated frames between them.

- It works with every method PushFPS runs itself: NVIDIA's, AMD's FSR and PushFlow. Not with Intel XeSS,
  which presents its own frames.
- It needs no setup and no warm-up, and it is its own switch: *Quick setup → Keep the HUD sharp*.
  Live HUD Lock is still there and is not changed by it.
- With a still camera or in a menu it touches nothing.
- It costs a little graphics card time for every frame (one pass over the picture). How much on your card
  is not measured yet: if frame generation skips more than before, switch it off and tell us.
- Measured on PushFlow's models with drawn test pictures: the HUD's pixels were off by 14 to 40 of 255
  without it and by about 1 with it.
- **What it does not fix:** ghosting in the moving world right beside a character. A character who stands
  still while the world moves keeps a cleaner outline (the error inside it roughly halves); the band of
  world next to him is as before, and so is a character who moves himself.
- A source with visible noise (an analogue link, a hard-compressed stream) has few pixels that are the same
  twice, so the rule finds less there.

### A steadier rhythm for 30 FPS games

With duplicate removal on *60 → 30* (a 30 FPS game through a 60 Hz capture), a game that dipped under 30
made PushFPS lose its rhythm: it kept a repeated picture, then ran three pictures 16.7 ms late and threw
one away. In one session we looked at, that happened 325 times, about once every 14 seconds.

A repeat of the picture already kept now waits for the next new picture instead. A still picture (a
menu, a loading screen) keeps its rhythm, and the first new picture after one is shown at once.

### Lip sync by hand

*Quick setup → Lip sync*: move the sound earlier or later in steps of 10 ms, up to 250 ms.

Automatic sync delays the sound by the time the picture spends inside PushFPS. It cannot see how much later
than its sound your capture card hands over the picture, or what your screen, soundbar or wireless headphones
add afterwards. If voices come before the mouths move, press *Later 10* until they match.

- It adds to automatic sync. In manual mode, or with sync off, it does nothing, and the row says so.
- Sound cannot be played before it arrives: when *Earlier 10* has no more effect, the row says so.

### A clear line in "compare to original"

The live split comparison now has a line where the two sides meet: white between two dark edges, so it
shows on bright and dark pictures. It is as high as the picture and scales with the resolution.

### Put the latency readout where you want it

With *Show latency on screen* on, drag the readout anywhere over the picture. It stays there after a
restart and when the window changes size, and it cannot leave the picture. A double click sends it back to
the top left corner. While mouse input goes to the game it cannot be moved.

### Capture cards

- **Sound for a new capture card.** A card that was never set up started with *Don't capture audio*, and
  the sound input had to be picked by hand. That is what the "no audio" reports came from. A new card now
  starts with its own sound input chosen, when it is beyond doubt which one that is. You see the choice in
  the capture dialog before you connect. A microphone or headset is never chosen.
- A card you already set up keeps its setting, also *Don't capture audio*. If its own input is found, the
  capture dialog offers it, and connecting without sound is said (three times at most).
- Reported working by users and added to the list: **Elgato HD60 Pro** and **ezcap Game Link Raw**.

### PS5 Remote Play

- A video frame that arrived at the very moment the queue was read could be taken for too old, which
  started a recovery (a short freeze and a new keyframe). It is now treated as new.
- Controller input: a quick press and its release between two sends both reach the console, analogue
  values use the newest reading, and an input older than 100 ms is dropped by itself instead of with
  everything behind it.
- The log now says what started each recovery.

### Video files and HDR

- AV1 files use the graphics card's AV1 decoder when it has one (software decoding as before when not).
- Dolby Vision profile 8.4 files whose base layer is marked wrongly are shown as HLG, as they should be.
- A display that reports the HDR format's limit (10,000 nits) instead of its own peak is no longer believed
  by the automatic tone curve.

### Also

- **Log files are named after PushFPS.** `pushfps-qml.log`, `pushfps-last-session.txt`,
  `pushfps-last-failure.txt`, `pushfps-crash-*.dmp`, `pushfps-hang-*.dmp`. Files from an older version
  are taken over once at the first start.
- **Performance report:** it now includes how the picture reaches the screen and the time from PushFPS
  handing a frame over to the refresh that shows it. The on-screen latency figure ends at the hand-over;
  this is the step after it.

### What was tested where

- **The Windows build's automatic tests** (software renderer, no real graphics card, no capture card,
  no console): every item above. For the HUD rule the three shaders are compared with the rule's plain
  code pixel by pixel; the 60 → 30 logic is replayed against the old one on recorded cadences; the sound
  input choice is tested on device names as Windows writes them.
- **Measured on PushFlow's shipped models with drawn pictures:** the HUD figures above.
- **Not tested by us:** any of it on a real NVIDIA, AMD or Intel card, on a real capture card, on a PS5,
  or with a real game. The line in the comparison and the dragged readout have not been seen on a real
  screen. FSR on AMD cards gets the HUD rule like every method, and nobody has looked at the result on an
  AMD card yet.

### Known limits

- Ghosting around a moving character, and in the world right beside a still one, is not improved.
- The capture device list that never answers on some PCs with two Elgato cards is still open: we need a
  log from 3.2.6 or newer to see where it stops.
