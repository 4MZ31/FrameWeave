## What's new in 3.1.3: PushFlow, measured quality, signed updates and your own layouts

**PushFlow: our own frame generation.** PushFlow runs RIFE, an open neural network for in-between frames, through DirectML, so it works on NVIDIA, AMD and Intel graphics cards alike.
- Pick it under *Frame generation* in Professional mode or in streaming mode.
- 2×, 3× or 4×, and a tier: **Fast** (lite model, motion at half resolution), **Balanced** (lite model, full resolution) or **Quality** (full model).
- Everything stays on the graphics card: the frames go into the network and back without copies through the CPU, and the models are half precision where that gives the same picture.
- It works with HUD protection, Live HUD Lock, the adaptive multiplier, recording and going live.
- TensorRT isn't bundled: its runtime is over a gigabyte and NVIDIA-only. DirectML covers every vendor.

**Measured picture quality.** The new **frame quality test** holds back every second real frame, lets frame generation rebuild it, and compares the result with the frame it held back:
- accuracy (PSNR) and pixel errors;
- smearing inside your HUD boxes;
- the gain over a simple blend of the two frames;
- whether generated frames arrive on time.

**Optimise for my PC** now uses these scores to rank frame generation methods on your own game, instead of a fixed order. Sources it can't test (hardware-decoded streams) keep the fixed order.

**PS5 latency test.** During PS5 Remote Play, PushFPS presses the PS button for you, waits for the control centre to appear, and shows how long it took. It splits the PS5, network and decoding part from PushFPS's own processing. Find it in Professional mode and streaming mode under *Measure*.

**One-click updates, signed.** From this version, new releases install from the app with one click. Every release is signed (RSA-3072, SHA-256), and the app checks the signature, the version and the download's hash before anything is replaced. Your settings stay.

**Frame input for other apps.** Other programs can send their video straight into PushFPS through a documented shared-memory interface (`docs/FRAME-INPUT-API.md`, with a small sample sender).

**Your own layout.** Streaming mode has a **Layout** editor that shows the output picture with the game and every source on it.
- Drag to move. Drag a corner to resize: sizes keep their proportions, and holding **Ctrl** stretches freely.
- Edges and the middle snap into place. The arrow keys nudge (hold Shift for bigger steps).
- Crop every side of the game picture or any source.
- Shrink the game picture into a box on a background colour.
- **Ctrl+Z** undoes your last change and **Ctrl+Y** (or Ctrl+Shift+Z) redoes it.
- Changes apply at once while you are live or recording.

**Separate stream and recording sizes.** The stream and recordings each have their own resolution: the picture's own size, 4K, 1440p, 1080p, 900p or 720p, or vertical 1080 × 1920 (recordings also 4:5 and square). When the shape differs from the game, **Fit** keeps the whole picture with bars, **Fill** crops the edges and **Stretch** distorts. Scene cards get bars instead of being stretched.

**Platform presets for going live.** Pick a platform at the resolution and frame rate you want, and the stream's size, bitrate, keyframe interval and audio come from that platform's own guidelines:
- **Twitch**: 1080p60 at 6 Mbps, 1080p30 and 720p60 at 4.5 Mbps, 720p30 at 3 Mbps, audio 160 kbps.
- **YouTube**: 2160p to 720p at YouTube's recommended H.264 bitrates, for example 1080p60 at 17 Mbps and 4K60 at 50 Mbps, audio 128 kbps.
- **Kick**: up to 1080p60 at 8 Mbps.
- **Facebook**: 1080p60 at 7.5 Mbps, inside Meta's 4.5–9 Mbps range.
- **TikTok LIVE**: vertical 1080 × 1920 or 720 × 1280.
- **Trovo**: up to 1080p60 at 8 Mbps.

You can still change everything afterwards: resolution, fit, bitrate (1–51 Mbps), 30 or 60 FPS, keyframe every 1–4 seconds, audio bitrate and the encoder preset. A preset you have changed shows *modified*.

**Upload presets for recordings.** Under *Settings, Playback*:
- **YouTube** from 4K to 720p at 30 or 60 FPS, at YouTube's recommended upload bitrates;
- **YouTube HDR** (HEVC 10-bit, NVIDIA);
- **YouTube Shorts**, **TikTok** and **Instagram Reels** at 1080 × 1920;
- **X** at 1080p or 720p;
- two for **editing**, at the picture's own size and constant quality.

TikTok, Reels and X publish no exact bitrate, so those presets use the commonly recommended 8–15 Mbps within each platform's limits.

**Also new:**
- **Built on Veyra**: PushFPS is now its own project. The sponsor page and its links are gone from the app.
- **Split files**: long recordings can start a new file every 30, 60 or 120 minutes without stopping.
- **Free placement**: sources can sit anywhere, not only in a corner.
- **Hardware encoders**: NVENC on NVIDIA; on AMD and Intel, the card's own hardware encoder.

**Everything from 3.1.1 and 3.1.2** is in this build too:
- webcam, window and video sources;
- desktop audio and an extra input;
- scene transitions;
- duplicate frames always removed before frame generation;
- the setup guide with targets for every monitor and the capture card section (**PushFPS-Setup-Guide.txt** is attached).

**How it was tested.** In CI on Windows, without a graphics card:
- the build and the unit tests: layout maths, output sizes, source cropping, the quality maths, the latency detector, the frame input protocol, and the update check (signature, tampered files, a full local download and install);
- the GPU tests on WARP (Windows' software renderer): PushFlow's models make in-between frames that beat a plain blend, plus the layout and output passes;
- the PushFlow models checked on the CPU when the release is built;
- the QML tests for the layout editor, the presets, the optimiser's measured ranking, frame input and the PushFlow picker in streaming mode;
- the install test, which opens every main page.

Not yet tested on real hardware: PushFlow's speed on NVIDIA, AMD and Intel cards, the quality test and the PS5 latency test on a live game, and one-click updates (3.1.4 will be the first release to update into). Reports are welcome.

**Windows Defender note.** Defender may flag new PushFPS downloads as `Trojan:Win32/Tecabans.STV!cl`. This is a false positive from its cloud check on new, unsigned files, and it has been reported to Microsoft. Always check the zip against `SHA256SUMS.txt` on this page.
