## What's new in 3.2.10: the last three PushFlow 2 switches

With this version every part of the PushFlow 2 plan has its switch. All of them **start off**, and with
all of them off PushFPS makes exactly the frames it made before (the tests compare them).

You find them in *Professional → Frame generation*, under the PushFlow tier. This version adds three.

**Dual: true frames for recordings and streams.** With Predict on (3.2.9), your screen shows forecasts, and
recordings, instant replay and streams recorded what was on screen, forecasts included. With Dual they get
the true picture instead: the real frames, and the true in-between frame of each pair. Predict has already
worked that frame out, so it costs almost nothing.

- What you see, and its delay, do not change.
- It acts only while Predict runs, at **2X**. At 3X and 4X recordings stay as played.
- The recording runs one frame behind the screen, and its sound is one frame ahead of its picture (about
  33 ms at 30 FPS).
- Comparison clips still record what is on screen.
- **No automatic test here makes an actual recording with it.** The frames handed to the recorder are
  checked; the file is not. Please look at one recording before you rely on it.

**Response: a measurement first.** The idea of Response is to turn the picture a little from the right
stick before the console's own frame arrives. Nobody has shown that this can be done from a video signal,
so the first step only measures, and changes nothing on the picture: does the picture's own motion follow
the right stick, how strongly, and how late?

- It works only where the controller goes through PushFPS: **PS5 Remote Play**. A controller plugged into
  the console cannot be seen, and the row says so.
- It looks at the right stick's two axes and at the picture's own motion (from PushFlow, while Predict
  runs). No button is read, nothing is written to disk, nothing is sent anywhere, and everything is
  cleared when you switch it off or change the source.
- Play for a few minutes and turn the camera a lot. The row then says whether the picture follows the
  stick, by how much and how late. That answer, from your game, is what decides whether Response is worth
  building further.

**Memory: a measurement first.** The idea of Memory is to bring back detail that earlier real frames
showed. That only helps where neighbouring real frames really differ, so the first step measures it: where
the motion is known, how far apart are two real frames once they are lined up? A capture card's picture
is not compressed and should hardly differ; a streamed picture should differ more. The row shows the
figure and what it means. Nothing on the picture changes.

Both measurements need Predict running, and both are in the performance report (*Settings → Performance
report*), so a report from your setup tells us what no test here can.

**If something goes wrong**, `PushFPS.exe --safe-mode` starts with every experiment off, for that run.

### What was tested, and how

- Built and tested automatically on Windows (GitHub's build machines, software renderer). Dual: through
  the whole frame generation pipeline (the recorder's frames are the real frame before and exactly the true
  in-between frame, for every pair) and through the whole engine with a live source (they are handed over
  at the right moments, and the screen is as with Predict alone). Response: its arithmetic on made-up
  sticks and pictures with a known answer, and the motion it reads from a panning and a still picture.
  Memory: the same picture twice, and a picture made slightly brighter.
- **Not tested on a real graphics card, capture card, console or controller**, and no recording was made
  with Dual. Response and Memory have never seen real input: finding out what they say on real setups is
  what this release is for.
- Nothing in capture, recording, streaming or the other frame generation methods changes for people who
  leave the switches off.

**Windows Defender note.** Defender may flag new PushFPS downloads as `Trojan:Win32/Tecabans.STV!cl`. This is a false positive from its cloud check on new, unsigned files, and it has been reported to Microsoft. Always check the zip against `SHA256SUMS.txt` on this page.
