## What's new in 3.2.3: comparison clips with sound again, smoother cameras, a scenes menu

### Comparison clips could come out silent

Two faults in Streaming mode's scenes caused it, and both reached every mode, Professional mode included,
because the scene and the audio mixer are shared by the whole program:

- A card scene (*Starting soon*, *Be right back*, *Thanks for watching*) mutes the game sound while it is
  shown. It did that by switching on the mixer's own saved *Game sound* mute. If PushFPS was closed while a
  card was selected, that mute stayed on for good: every later recording, comparison clip, instant replay
  and stream had no game sound, while you still heard the game normally.
- A comparison clip never shows the card (it always shows the game, original against PushFPS), but it was
  still silenced by one. With a card left selected, a comparison clip looked right and had no sound.

What changed:

- **Nothing to do after updating.** The first time 3.2.3 starts, a game mute or a faded-out game fader left
  in the mixer is put back, with a message. If you had muted the game on purpose, mute it again in
  *Streaming mode → Audio mixer*.
- A card's mute is now its own, temporary thing. It is never saved, it ends when you leave the card, and the
  mixer's *Game sound* mute is only ever changed by you.
- Comparison clips ignore a card's mute. The mixer's own fader and mute still apply to them.
- Starting a recording or a comparison clip now tells you when it will not hold what you expect: the game
  or the microphone is muted in the mixer, or (outside Streaming mode) a card scene is the picture.
- In Streaming mode the *Game sound* strip shows when a card is muting it, and its mute button lifts that
  too.

### Cameras in Streaming mode

- **The camera's picture in PushFPS is live.** Under a source's settings and in the layout editor the
  picture was a still, fetched twice a second (once a second in the editor), so every camera looked like a
  slide show whatever it really ran at. It now follows the camera frame for frame.
- **Less work per frame.** Rounded and round sources computed a distance for every pixel of every frame;
  only the pixels on the edge are computed now, with the same result pixel for pixel. A frame is also
  fitted to the size its layer is shown before it goes to the recorder, so a camera in a corner no longer
  sends a full-size picture across for every frame.
- **Resolution is a choice**: *Auto* (720p, as before), *1080p*, *1440p* or *4K* under the camera's
  settings. The camera gives the closest size it offers, and the list under the choice shows what that
  is. Many cameras send less in webcam mode than they record (a camera that records 4K often sends 1080p
  over USB), so a 4K choice shows 1080p with such a camera: that is all it sends.
- The line under a source's name shows what the source sends and its real frame rate, in amber under
  15 FPS. The log (`push-sources`) records the format a camera was opened with, everything it offers, and
  every ten seconds its rate and the time a frame takes.

### Scenes

- **Right-click the scenes list** (as in OBS): on a scene to switch to it, edit, duplicate or delete it;
  beside the scenes to add a game scene or a card scene. A duplicate sits right under the original with
  its sources.
- Deleting the game scene that is on now, with another game scene left, switches to that one (it could
  leave the deleted scene selected).

**How it was tested.** In CI on Windows without a graphics card or a camera: the build, the unit tests
(the mixer's gains with and without a card; the camera format choice; the fitting and colour conversion;
the edge-only shape against the every-pixel version at eighteen sizes, pixel for pixel), the QML tests of
Streaming mode (scenes, the menu, the mixer, sources) and the install test. Measured on a Linux build
machine, not on Windows: shaping a rounded 1080p frame went from 5.6 ms to 0.02 ms.

**Not tested on real hardware.** No comparison clip was recorded with a capture card for this release, and
the cause of the silent clips was found by reading the code, not reproduced on the PC of the report. No
camera was connected at any point: CI has none, so the camera code (opening a format, 4K, the live
picture) has only been compiled and unit tested, never run against a device. If a camera is still choppy,
the line under its name and the `push-sources` lines in the log now say what it sends and how fast. The log
line written when a recording closes says how much game sound it was given and how much the mixer silenced
(`soundBlocks`, `mixerSilenced`), so a silent clip can be told apart at once if this was not all of it.

**Windows Defender note.** Defender may flag new PushFPS downloads as `Trojan:Win32/Tecabans.STV!cl`. This is a false positive from its cloud check on new, unsigned files, and it has been reported to Microsoft. Always check the zip against `SHA256SUMS.txt` on this page.
