## What's new in 4.2

Four things people asked for: comparison clips that look as good as the picture itself, long recordings
that are split into parts without losing a frame, a quick tour for new users, and Korean.

### Comparison clips: choose the picture quality

A comparison clip is made to be looked at closely, so it should not show blocks in fast motion. The
comparison clip dialog (and *Settings → Recording → Comparison clips*) has a new row, **Quality**:

- **Standard**: the standard bitrate, as every clip was until now. Smaller files, good for sharing
  straight away.
- **High** (the default from now on): encoded at a fixed quality instead of a fixed bitrate. Detail stays
  clear and the picture clean however busy the scene gets; the file's size follows what is on screen.
- **Near lossless**: almost impossible to tell from the picture itself. Files are large (a 4K clip can
  take several GB a minute), so record to an SSD.

It works for every layout: split, side by side, vertical and reveal.

For a 4K clip that looks lossless, choose **Clip size → 4K** and **Quality → Near lossless** (clip size is
for the split and reveal layouts). That needs a strong graphics card: if frames could not be recorded, you
are told when the clip ends, and one step lower is the answer.

The row that was called "quality" before chooses the recorded height and is now called **Clip size**.

### Recordings in parts, without a gap

A long recording can continue in a new file every so many minutes or once a file reaches a size
(*Settings → Recording → Split files* and *Split by size*). Until now a few frames and a moment of sound
were lost at every change of file.

Now nothing is lost. One encoder runs through the whole recording; a part ends at a keyframe and the next
part starts with that keyframe. Put the parts one after another in an editor and they join seamlessly: no
picture and no sound is missing or doubled.

- A part split by size ends just before that size, never after it.
- Every finished part is a standard MP4 that plays and opens in an editor by itself.
- Clip markers are still written per file, with times from the start of that part.
- A recording that is not split is written exactly as before.

### A quick tour for new users

On a new install PushFPS offers a one-minute tour: where to connect the capture card, what each choice in
the capture dialog means (device, resolution, frame rate, format, colour), then the console and game frame
rate, the target frame rate, AI picture enhancement and the tools. Each step lights up the control it
talks about.

*Esc* leaves it at any time, and **Open the quick tour** at the bottom of the home page opens it again.
If you update from an earlier version, the tour does not start by itself.

### Korean

PushFPS is now in Korean: 15 languages in all. On a Korean Windows it starts in Korean; otherwise choose
it in *Settings → Language*. The Linux and macOS preview has it too.

Built on Veyra 2.2.0: the tour's spotlight, the Korean of the engine's own texts and the way a recording
is cut at a keyframe come from it.
