## What's new in 3.1.6: PushFlow at 4K, steadier comparison clips

**PushFlow is fast enough at 4K.** At a 4K picture every PushFlow tier ran the neural network at 4K (Fast at 1080p). That was too slow for live play, so PushFPS skipped every frame pair it could not finish in time: PushFlow stayed at the game's own 30 FPS, and *Optimise for my PC* saw no generated frames while testing it. Now motion is estimated at up to 960×540 (Fast), 1280×720 (Balanced) or 1920×1080 (Quality), and the picture is rebuilt at full resolution from the real frames, the same way the Fast tier has kept detail since 3.1.5. On a 1080p picture Quality still runs at full size.

**Comparison clips don't freeze.** Split and side-by-side comparison clips recorded every frame at the picture's full size, which at 4K and 180 FPS is more than a hardware encoder can take. Frames were dropped, so the console side stuttered, froze, or showed a picture from seconds earlier. These clips are now recorded at up to 1440p (vertical clips stay 1080×1920).

**The right label shows what you see.** The PushFPS label used the configured multiplier ceiling, so a 60 FPS target with a 3X ceiling read "180 FPS". It now uses the multiplier actually in use, and never goes above the output cap or the display.

**Live play as smooth as the recording.** Choosing a 60 FPS (or 120 FPS) target switched *Smooth frame pacing* off, because the switch also meant "cap at the display". Without it, Windows replaced frames that hadn't been shown yet, so the recording had every frame but the screen skipped some: micro-jitter and a lower frame rate live. Smooth frame pacing now works with a target. It turns on once for anyone whose display sync was still on Automatic, and it warns when the target doesn't divide your refresh rate evenly (60 FPS on a 144 Hz screen without G-SYNC/FreeSync).

**30 FPS games seen as 60.** Some capture cards add a little noise to the repeated pictures of a 30 FPS game in a 60 Hz signal, so automatic duplicate removal sees a new picture every time. Frame generation then works from 60 FPS, and comparison clips label the console side "60 FPS" (reported with GTA V's fidelity mode). When the game frame rate is set to 30 but 60 is measured, the quick setup now says so and offers the fixed *60→30* duplicate removal in one tap.

**How it was tested.** In CI on Windows without a graphics card: the build, the unit tests, PushFlow's whole path on WARP (Windows' software renderer, including the new network sizes), comparison clips on WARP, the QML tests and the install test. Not tested on real hardware yet: PushFlow's speed at 4K and comparison clips with a capture card.

**Windows Defender note.** Defender may flag new PushFPS downloads as `Trojan:Win32/Tecabans.STV!cl`. This is a false positive from its cloud check on new, unsigned files, and it has been reported to Microsoft. Always check the zip against `SHA256SUMS.txt` on this page.
